Method

An annual model, kept deliberately transparent: every figure on the other tabs comes from the formulas below, and the source of each is a plain JavaScript function you can read in js/model.js.

Structure

  • All-in rate — fixed tranches pay their coupon; floating tranches pay base rate + spread, floored at zero. Undrawn revolver pays a 0.35% commitment fee.
  • Net leverage = (total debt − cash) ÷ LTM EBITDA. Interest cover = EBITDA ÷ cash interest.
  • Leverage through a tranche — cumulative debt down to and including that tranche, in turns of EBITDA, the way term sheets quote it.
  • Indicative band — maps net leverage and interest cover to a letter band using round-number thresholds and takes the weaker of the two. It is a teaching heuristic, not a rating and not a substitute for an agency's methodology.

Projection

  • EBITDA grows at the chosen rate from LTM, with the shock applied from year one onward.
  • Interest accrues on opening balances. Cash tax = tax rate × (EBITDA − D&A − interest), floored at zero.
  • Free cash flow = EBITDA − interest − tax − capex − change in working capital and other.
  • Scheduled amortisation is paid each year. With the refinancing market open, maturing debt rolls for five years on the same terms; shut, it must be repaid from cash, then the revolver.
  • Cash below the minimum is topped up from the revolver while it is available. Anything still short is a liquidity shortfall and counts as a breach.
  • Excess cash above the minimum is swept, at the chosen percentage, to the revolver first and then to floating-rate debt by seniority.

Recovery

  • Distressed EV = distressed EBITDA × multiple. Administrative and priority claims come off the top.
  • The remainder pays claims by rank — first lien (with the revolver), second lien, senior unsecured, subordinated — pro rata within a rank. Claims are principal only; accrued interest and make-wholes are ignored.
  • The fulcrum is the most senior class that is not paid in full.

What it leaves out

  • Intra-year timing, hedging, PIK toggles, EBITDA add-backs, equity cures, springing-covenant tests and deficiency claims of secured lenders against unsecured value.
  • Presets are invented companies built to show three typical credit profiles. They do not describe any real issuer.
